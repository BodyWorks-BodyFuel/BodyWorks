# BodyWorks Experience North Star

## Purpose

BodyWorks helps people follow a familiar food into a living body and understand how repeated food patterns take shape over time.

The experience is not primarily a calorie calculator, nutrition dashboard, diet planner, or fitness tracker. It is an interactive explanation of:

**Food → nutrient resources → the body → response → patterns over time**

## Core promise

**See what food brings into a living body—and what repetition changes over time.**

## Canonical story

### 1. Begin with food

Start with something immediately recognizable: a banana, oatmeal, avocado, chicken, pasta, or another familiar food.

The first question is not:

> How many calories is this?

It is:

> What does this food bring into the body?

### 2. Reveal what the food contributes

Show the food separating into the resources represented by the model:

- Protein
- Carbohydrates
- Fat
- Energy
- Other supported nutrient information when relevant

Calories remain visible, but as part of this resource description—not as the headline or a judgment.

### 3. Follow those resources into the body

The selected food should visibly initiate the experience.

Food becomes nutrient resources. Those resources enter the body, travel through connected pathways, and contribute differently to ongoing biological processes.

Motion begins with the food and travels directionally into the body.

### 4. Show that different foods bring different mixtures

Different foods contribute different proportions and combinations of resources.

The experience should demonstrate difference without grading foods as good or bad. It should encourage comparison, curiosity, and understanding rather than correction.

### 5. Introduce time deliberately

One food represents one eating moment.

Several intentionally selected foods can become an example day.

Repeated example days reveal tendencies across:

- Today
- Days
- Weeks
- Months

Time should be experienced through repetition, persistence, accumulation, and changing emphasis—not merely described with text or brighter colors.

### 6. Deliver the conceptual payoff

**Food becomes available resources.  
Resources enter a living body.  
Repeated moments become patterns over time.**

## Role of calories

Calories are scientifically relevant, but they are not the protagonist. Calories describe one dimension of food: energy.

They should:

- Appear after a food has been selected.
- Describe part of what the food contributes.
- Provide energy context without defining the food's entire meaning.
- Avoid comparison with a full-day reference until the visitor has deliberately created a complete example day.
- Never become a score, moral judgment, or shorthand for whether a food is good or bad.

## Experience principles

### Demonstrate before explaining

Let the visitor see a transformation before presenting detailed instruction.

### Reveal one layer at a time

Do not show the entire model before the visitor has a reason to understand it.

### Every motion must communicate meaning

Motion should represent transformation, direction, routing, repetition, or persistence. Ambient animation must remain secondary.

### Begin concrete and move toward abstraction

Food is concrete. Nutrients are the first abstraction. Body routing is the system. Time reveals the pattern.

### Preserve scientific restraint

The experience represents generalized relationships and tendencies—not measurement, diagnosis, individualized prediction, or medical advice.

### Maintain warmth without losing credibility

Familiar food provides the human entry point. The body model provides depth. Calm presentation preserves trust.

## Current-version boundaries

This version includes:

- Food
- Nutrient and energy contributions
- The body's interconnected processes
- Storage, use, repair, and other supported system responses
- Eating moments and repeated food patterns
- Change across time

This version does not expand into:

- Exercise or workout tracking
- Sleep
- Stress or mood
- Symptom interpretation
- General lifestyle optimization
- Medical diagnosis
- Personalized outcome prediction
- Diet prescription
- Food scoring or moral judgment

## Decision filter

Before adding or changing anything, ask:

1. Does it begin with or remain clearly connected to food?
2. Does it help reveal what food brings into the body?
3. Does it make a bodily relationship easier to see?
4. Does it clarify the difference between a moment and a pattern over time?
5. Does it demonstrate something instead of merely adding information?
6. Does it remain nonjudgmental and scientifically restrained?
7. Is it within the food–body–time boundary?

If the answer is no, it is probably outside the current path.

## Guiding sentence

**Start with food. Reveal its resources. Follow them through the body. Let time reveal the pattern.**
